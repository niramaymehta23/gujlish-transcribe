# Harisena clip rules

Draft 1. These will be updated over time. When the channel owner corrects a choice, add the lesson here.

## Audience

Teenagers who are mildly spiritual, not deeply spiritual. Also anyone not deeply involved with satsang who has some
spiritual awakening: a mumukshu (a sincere seeker). Assume they do not know the terms, the people or the history.

## What the talks are

Pravachans and goshthis of Guruhari Prabodh Swamiji. Everything will be spiritual already, so being spiritual is not
enough. The question is whether it would stop this audience from scrolling and stay with them.

## Clip types

Use these exact names as the clip's `type`.

- **analogy**: an everyday comparison that explains a spiritual idea. The model example: you see a doctor for a physical
  illness and a psychiatrist for a mental one, but who cures ego, jealousy, worry, sleeplessness and bad thoughts? Those
  are illnesses of the soul, and for those you need a true saint, who acts as a doctor. That is why we need satsang.
- **story**: a story, prasang or anecdote with a clear beginning and a point.
- **prabhudasbhai**: any anecdote about Prabhudasbhai. That was the name of Guruhari Hariprasad Swamiji, the guru of
  Guruhari Prabodh Swamiji, before diksha, so these are stories of his youth. **Very highly recommended: score these
  high when they stand on their own.**
- **agna**: a direct command or message the audience can act on: no movies, listen to your parents, study well, hard work
  is a must, and similar.
- **regional-saint**: agnas about the Pradeshik Sant (regional saint), or about following a saint such as Brahmavihari
  Swami.
- **description**: a vivid description or explanation that holds attention even without a story.

## Every clip must

- Be relevant to Harisena: right for the teenage, mildly spiritual audience above.
- Be catchy: the opening seconds make someone want to stay.
- Make sense on its own, without the rest of the talk. Avoid openings that depend on earlier context ("as I said",
  "because of that").
- Start where a thought begins and end when it lands, not mid-idea.

## Avoid

- Long stretches of chanting, jai calls, greetings, announcements or thanks.
- Passages that assume deep knowledge of scripture or of satsang history, unless the speaker explains them.
- Anything that needs the audience in the room to make sense.
- Passages where the translation looks garbled or names look wrong. Mention this under `concerns` if the clip is still
  worth considering.
- Crude or bodily humour (constipation and the like), even when the room laughed. (Rejected Nov 2026.)
- Blunt images of death: a dead body, "take him away", the cremation and last rites. (Rejected Nov 2026.)

If a strong passage has one of these in the middle, keep the passage and cut the bad part out with `skip`. The
channel owner liked the happiness ladder (bicycle, scooter, car, US visa) but not the joke that followed it.

## Scoring

- **9-10**: a Prabhudasbhai anecdote, or an analogy or story with a real hook that a teenager would send to a friend.
- **7-8**: a clear, direct agna or a strong description with a good opening.
- **5-6**: good content, but a slow opening, or it needs some outside context.
- **Below 5**: leave it out.

Do not reward length. A tight 50 seconds beats a loose 2:30.

## Formats

- **short**: for YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels.
- **long**: a longer passage for a normal YouTube upload, when it needs the extra time and holds up for all of it.
